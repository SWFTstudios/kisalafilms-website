The work

Coverage where it counts

Most bikes do not need every panel changed to look different. The tank, the tail and the front fairing carry the shape, and wrapping those three is what people register.

Common jobs
Tank only, tail section, front fairing, belly pan, mirror caps, fender, racing stripes, and chrome delete on the trim.
Why it works
A satin black tank against factory bodywork reads as deliberate. A half-finished full wrap does not, which is why the panel choice matters more than the film.
Finishes
Same catalogue as a full wrap — gloss, satin, matte, metallic, carbon and colour shift.
Turnaround
Usually two to three days depending on how much comes off the bike.

Included

What comes with it

Panel choice, worked out first

We decide together which surfaces to cover. Getting this wrong is the difference between a build and a bike with mismatched bodywork.

Chrome delete

Mirror stalks, trim strips and badges taken to satin or gloss black, either on their own or alongside accent panels.

Edges wrapped, not trimmed

Panels come off for the same reason they do on a full wrap. A cut edge on the visible face is where lifting starts.

Room to grow

Accents done in a catalogued film can be extended to the rest of the bike later without hunting for a colour match.

Know which panels you want?

Tell me the bike and point at the parts. If a full wrap is better value for what you are after, I will say so.
