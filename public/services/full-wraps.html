The work

What a full wrap actually involves

The bike comes apart before any film goes on. Fairings, tank and tail come off so each panel can be wrapped as its own piece and the film can turn the edge and sit on the back.

Coverage
Fairings, tank, tail, belly pan, fenders and side panels — every painted surface on the bike.
Film
Cast vinyl only. Calendared film shrinks back off compound curves within a season and is not worth the labour it sits on.
Finishes
Gloss, satin, matte, metallic, chrome, carbon and textured, colour shift. Around a thousand options in the catalog.
Turnaround
Several days with the bike in the garage. Rushing a full wrap is how you get lifting edges.

What comes with it

Disassembly and reassembly

Panels come off and go back on. Wrapping around fasteners rather than removing them is what produces the seams you see on cheap work.

Tank PPF on wear zones

Clear protection where your knees, boots and jacket zip meet the tank, so the film underneath survives being ridden.

Design and material consult

We pick the film together and look at it in daylight. Screens are unreliable about metallics and colour shifts.

Photos of your bike

Shot when it comes off the stand. Whether any of it goes public is your call, not a condition of the price.

Straight answers

Before you book one

A wrap is not paint and pretending otherwise costs you money later.

It protects the paint, it does not repair it

Film hides a chip. It does not fix one, and it will not stick well over flaking clear coat. If the paint is failing, that gets sorted first.

It comes off

Cast film lifts cleanly from factory paint with heat, which is most of the point — the bike goes back to standard whenever you want it to.

Not every panel is worth wrapping

Deeply recessed vents and some textured plastics will not hold film honestly. I will say so rather than take the money and let it lift.

Ready to change the whole bike?

Send the year, make and model with a few photos and I’ll come back with a real number.
